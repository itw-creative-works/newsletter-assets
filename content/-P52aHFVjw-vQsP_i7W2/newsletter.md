# Attention beats impressions, and search is not enough

_Two practical shifts for better funnel planning and scan-driven campaigns._

Marketers love easy numbers, but easy numbers can hide weak performance. This issue is about the two questions that matter more: did people actually pay attention, and did your funnel create demand before they were ready to search?

---

## Impressions are not proof of impact

![A clean editorial illustration of a crowded digital screen filled with ad tiles, with only one ad tile sharply highlighted and a QR code on a poster in the foreground, modern flat style.](https://cdn.itwcreativeworks.com/newsletters/dashqr/content/-P52aHFVjw-vQsP_i7W2/section-1.png)

A million impressions can sound impressive and still tell you very little about whether anyone noticed your ad. Views and reach are useful for distribution, but they do not prove attention, recall, or persuasion. That matters because people move quickly between screens, apps, and tasks, which means an ad can be served and still never really land. A useful mindset shift is to treat impressions as the start of the story, not the finish line. If you are promoting a poster, flyer, menu, or package with a QR code, the real question is whether the code is seen clearly enough to earn the scan, not just whether it was printed on a thousand pieces of collateral.

## Measure attention, not just delivery

![An overhead scene of a restaurant table, retail shelf, and flyer layout with a branded QR code centered and subtle heatmap-style focus zones showing where a viewer's eye lands, realistic design.](https://cdn.itwcreativeworks.com/newsletters/dashqr/content/-P52aHFVjw-vQsP_i7W2/section-2.png)

The strongest campaigns are built around what people actually absorb. If a person barely glances at an ad, that exposure is a lot less valuable than one that holds their focus long enough to register the message. For QR campaigns, this means paying attention to placement, contrast, size, and context. A code on a menu or shelf talker should be easy to spot, but also feel like part of the design so people do not ignore it as clutter. Tracking scans is helpful, but scan rate alone still misses the quality of the moment. Pair scan data with landing page engagement, time on page, and follow-on actions so you can see whether the attention translated into behavior.

## Search works better after demand exists

![A marketing funnel illustration that starts with social posts, event signage, and printed materials feeding into a search bar and conversion icons, using crisp corporate infographic styling.](https://cdn.itwcreativeworks.com/newsletters/dashqr/content/-P52aHFVjw-vQsP_i7W2/section-3.png)

It is tempting to pour more money into search when lead volume dips, but search usually works best when other channels have already done the hard part of making the brand familiar. Social, video, events, direct mail, and even in-person signage can introduce a brand long before someone types a query. If you only fund bottom-funnel capture, you may end up buying clicks from people who were already close to converting anyway. QR codes are useful here because they connect offline touchpoints to a measurable digital path. Put them on packaging, event signage, business cards, or flyers so you can see which physical placements are actually feeding downstream intent.

## Build a funnel that earns the next search

![A sleek funnel graphic showing a branded flyer with a QR code at the top, then a landing page, then a search result and conversion at the bottom, with warm brand colors and minimal icons.](https://cdn.itwcreativeworks.com/newsletters/dashqr/content/-P52aHFVjw-vQsP_i7W2/section-4.png)

The best lead gen plans do not treat channels as separate silos. They build memory and trust first, then make it easy for people to take the next step when they are ready. That can mean using branded QR codes on print collateral to drive traffic to a focused landing page, then comparing versions to see which message creates the most engagement. It can also mean reviewing where people first encountered the brand before they searched for it. If a scan comes from a flyer but the conversion happens after a second visit from branded search, both touchpoints matter. The goal is not to credit everything to search. The goal is to make sure search is harvesting demand you actually helped create.
