LinkedIn is still the place to find buyers
That matters for creators, consultants, and small brands because it means your posts are not just floating into the void.

If you are trying to reach people who actually influence budgets, LinkedIn is hard to ignore. The network now stretches across more than a billion members worldwide, and its audience includes a huge share of executives and other business decision-makers. They can land in front of people who buy tools, hire vendors, and share content internally. The play here is not to treat LinkedIn like a résumé site. Treat it like a distribution channel for proof, point of view, and practical wins you can repeat every week.

Video deserves a real slot in your cadence
Video is becoming one of LinkedIn’s more important formats, and that is a cue to stop burying it under text-only posts.

Short explainers, screen recordings, quick demos, and founder updates can do a lot of the heavy lifting because they show your process, not just your claims. You do not need a studio setup. You need a repeatable format that you can batch in one sitting and repurpose into clips, captions, and follow-up posts. If you already use automation, this is a good place to build a simple content ladder: one recording becomes a native video, a text post, a comment prompt, and a newsletter snippet.

Employee content is a trust shortcut, if you manage it well
People often trust posts from employees more than polished brand-page updates, which makes sense.

Real operators sound more specific, more immediate, and less scripted. For brands, that creates an easy win and a real governance challenge. The best approach is to give employees a clear lane: what they can share, how to disclose partnerships, and where to ask before posting sensitive material. You do not need to control every sentence. You do need guardrails, templates, and a lightweight review process so your team can post quickly without creating avoidable messes. When done right, employee content expands reach without turning your brand voice into a free-for-all.

Governance is now part of growth, not separate from it
That means centralized account access, clear approval rules, a plan for AI usage, and a backup path when someone leaves the team.

The social teams that move fastest usually do not skip process. They compress it. Those basics sound boring until a hacked account, a missed compliance issue, or a messy vendor handoff eats your week. If you are a solo marketer, start small: document logins, define who can publish, and make sure your automation tools are tied to one clean workflow. Good governance is what lets you scale without turning every post into a fire drill.
