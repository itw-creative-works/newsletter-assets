# How brands show up in AI answers

_A practical look at visibility, originality, and the new discovery loop._

AI search is changing how people discover brands, but the pattern is not random. The names that keep surfacing tend to be the ones with broad visibility, clear category fit, and enough original content around them for the model and the audience to recognize.

---

## Why some brands keep getting named first

![A clean editorial illustration of a digital storefront skyline where a few brand logos glow brighter than the rest, in a modern flat style.](https://cdn.itwcreativeworks.com/newsletters/somiibo/content/-P6D9RgGBP7pVi1U3MwG/section-1.png)

If you look at AI-generated shopping advice, a handful of brands show up over and over. The pattern is pretty simple: categories with strong everyday utility, broad distribution, and lots of web mentions tend to win the recall game. That matters for creators and small brands too. You may not outspend the big players, but you can make your niche easier to understand. Tight positioning, consistent naming, and repeated references across your site, posts, captions, and profiles help build the kind of footprint AI systems can pick up later. Think less about one perfect post and more about steady, recognizable signals across channels.

## Share of voice is now a real growth metric

![A dashboard-style illustration showing a pie chart, mention counters, and social icons on a laptop screen, with a focused builder vibe.](https://cdn.itwcreativeworks.com/newsletters/somiibo/content/-P6D9RgGBP7pVi1U3MwG/section-2.png)

Share of voice is just your mentions divided by the total mentions in your category, but it is more useful than it sounds. If your visibility is rising faster than your competitors, that is often a sign you are building momentum before the sales show up. For solo creators and small teams, this can be tracked in a lightweight way: count brand mentions, monitor social replies, watch search appearances, and compare yourself against a short list of direct competitors. The point is not to chase every mention. It is to see whether your content system is creating enough consistent presence to stay in the conversation week after week.

## Original content is what cuts through now

![A creator at a desk remixing one central idea into multiple content formats shown as cards, clips, and captions, in a bright illustrated style.](https://cdn.itwcreativeworks.com/newsletters/somiibo/content/-P6D9RgGBP7pVi1U3MwG/section-3.png)

The feed is crowded with lookalike posts, reused hooks, and generic AI copy, which is exactly why original content still matters. Audiences want brands to keep up with trends, but they also respond to a point of view that feels specific. That means the goal is not to avoid trends entirely. It is to use them as a wrapper for something only you can say. A practical workflow is to turn one original idea into several formats: a short reel, a carousel, a text post, and a reply thread. The message stays yours, but each version fits a different channel and gives you more chances to be noticed without sounding recycled.

## Use AI to remove editing friction, not your voice

![A video editor timeline with AI-assisted tools highlighted, beside a creator reviewing final cuts on a monitor, in a polished digital illustration.](https://cdn.itwcreativeworks.com/newsletters/somiibo/content/-P6D9RgGBP7pVi1U3MwG/section-4.png)

AI video tools are getting good at the annoying parts of production: trimming rough cuts, adding motion accents, and suggesting music choices. That is useful, especially if editing has been the bottleneck between ideas and posting. The trick is to treat AI like a junior editor, not a replacement for taste. Let it handle setup and cleanup, then make the final calls on pacing, structure, and tone. For Somiibo users, this pairs well with automation: generate the asset, schedule the post, then recycle the strongest clip into other networks. Less manual work, more published output, and a feed that still feels like a person made it.

## A simple workflow for this week

![A simple checklist flowing into a content automation pipeline, with icons for posting, repurposing, and analytics, in a clean startup illustration.](https://cdn.itwcreativeworks.com/newsletters/somiibo/content/-P6D9RgGBP7pVi1U3MwG/section-5.png)

If you want a practical starting point, keep it small. First, pick one niche topic you want to own and make sure your bios, captions, and pinned posts all say the same thing. Second, publish one original post series built around your perspective, not a recycled trend. Third, set up a repeatable repurposing loop so each piece can become multiple assets across TikTok, Instagram, X, and elsewhere. Fourth, track your mentions and engagement weekly so you can see whether visibility is moving. Automation helps here because it turns good habits into a system you can actually sustain.
