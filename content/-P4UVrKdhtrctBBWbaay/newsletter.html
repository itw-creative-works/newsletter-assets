WordPress blocks just got much simpler

If you have ever wanted a custom WordPress block without dragging in React, a build step, or a pile of packages, this change is worth a look. The new workflow trims block development down to the part most builders already know: PHP.
01
Story 01 of 04
 

One registration step instead of two
Less duplication means fewer things to break when you are trying to move fast.

The big shift here is simple: you can register a block in PHP and let WordPress handle the client-side wiring for you. In the old setup, a block usually needed matching PHP and JavaScript definitions, which meant more files, more upkeep, and more chances for the two sides to drift apart. Now the server-side registration can generate the editor-side behavior automatically. That is a real quality-of-life upgrade for small teams, agencies, and anyone maintaining a lot of custom blocks.

A practical fit for builders who do not want a frontend stack
This matters because a lot of WordPress work is not about building a rich app, it is about shipping a useful block quickly.

If your goal is a testimonial card, a callout, or a custom CTA, having to spin up a JS toolchain can feel like overkill. PHP-only registration lowers the barrier for developers who are comfortable in the back end but do not want to manage bundlers or modern frontend dependencies. It also makes custom block development feel closer to the rest of WordPress, where small, focused changes can live in a theme or plugin without much ceremony.

What this means for form-heavy sites
A lighter block workflow makes it easier to create custom form wrappers, landing page sections, and reusable content modules without building a full app around them.

For builders using forms as part of a broader WordPress site, this kind of simplification is useful. That pairs well with the kind of backend-first setup Slapform is built for: collect submissions, route them to email, webhooks, Zapier, or spreadsheets, and keep the front end lean. When the CMS side gets simpler, it is easier to keep the whole stack focused on output instead of maintenance.

The real win is less glue code
Fewer registration layers means a cleaner mental model for the people maintaining the site, especially when multiple builders touch the same project over time.

This update is not just about convenience. It reduces the glue code that usually sits between content editors and frontend behavior. It also nudges WordPress a little closer to the kind of workflow people expect from modern developer tools: define the block once, keep the implementation readable, and let the platform do more of the repetitive work. That is the kind of change that pays off every time you add a new block.
