# AI models are getting more capable, and more local

_From math milestones to leaner tools and tighter open-model gaps_

This issue is about AI getting a little more practical and a little less flashy. The pattern is pretty clear: better performance, smaller gaps, and more ways to use models without sending every task to the most expensive option.

---

## The smartest move might be the cheapest model

![A clean editorial illustration of a student dashboard routing different homework tasks to different sized AI models, with simple icons for easy, medium, and hard tasks.](https://cdn.itwcreativeworks.com/newsletters/studymonkey/content/-Oy5igraPpgGDVjLLpsO/section-1.png)

A lot of AI teams are starting to treat model choice like a smart schedule, not a default setting. Simple questions can go to lighter models, while harder tasks get the heavy hitters. That approach can trim costs without making the whole system worse, which matters if you are using AI for homework help, coding practice, or repeated note-taking. The lesson for students is useful too: not every problem needs the same effort. Start with the simplest tool that can handle it, then level up only when the task actually calls for it. That is basically good study habits in AI form.

## Open models are catching up in real ways

![A split-screen illustration showing two racetrack lanes labeled open and closed models, with the open lane nearly catching up on a technical course.](https://cdn.itwcreativeworks.com/newsletters/studymonkey/content/-Oy5igraPpgGDVjLLpsO/section-2.png)

The gap between open and closed models is not disappearing everywhere, but it is narrowing in specific technical areas. In security-style tests, some open models are now landing close to frontier systems that came out only a few months earlier. That does not mean every open model is the best choice for everything, but it does mean students and builders have more solid options than they did a year ago. If you are experimenting with AI for class projects, that is good news: you can often do a lot with tools that run locally, cost less, or give you more control over your data.

## Running AI on your own Mac is getting easier

![A modern MacBook on a desk showing a local AI chat app, with subtle desktop UI panels and a cozy student workspace.](https://cdn.itwcreativeworks.com/newsletters/studymonkey/content/-Oy5igraPpgGDVjLLpsO/section-3.png)

Local AI used to feel like a hobby project with a lot of setup. Now some desktop apps are packaging model chat and a local server into a single Mac-friendly workflow. That makes it much simpler to test models, reuse what is already on your machine, and keep certain tasks offline. For students, that can be useful when you want to draft, summarize, or brainstorm without juggling browser tabs or burning through credits. It is also a reminder that AI tools are becoming less like one giant website and more like a set of everyday utilities you can actually keep on your laptop.

## Math keeps delivering wild milestones

![An inspiring classroom-style illustration of a student and an AI tutor working through a math proof together on a whiteboard, with tidy handwritten steps.](https://cdn.itwcreativeworks.com/newsletters/studymonkey/content/-Oy5igraPpgGDVjLLpsO/section-4.png)

One of the most interesting recent headlines from the research world is a math breakthrough that would have sounded impossible not long ago. It shows that model progress is not just about chatting better or writing cleaner prose. Models are becoming more serious problem-solvers in domains where step-by-step reasoning really matters. For homework, that matters a lot. A model that can help you untangle a tricky proof, explain where an algebra step went sideways, or walk through a chemistry calculation is more than a novelty. It is a study partner, as long as you use it to understand the process instead of skipping it.
