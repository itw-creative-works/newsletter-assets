# Promotion is part of the work

_A practical case for planning distribution before you post_

I keep coming back to a simple question: if an idea is worth building, why do so many teams treat distribution like an afterthought? The smarter move is usually to decide creation and promotion together, then match the format to the channel and the audience you actually want.

---

## If it is worth making, it is worth aiming

![A builder's desk with a content brief, target audience notes, and channel checklist laid out beside a laptop, clean editorial illustration style.](https://cdn.itwcreativeworks.com/newsletters/somiibo/content/-OzDmca_UyrA07weR_iz/section-1.png)

There is a comforting habit in content marketing: make the thing first, then figure out how to share it later. That works until you need results, because “later” usually means vague targeting, fuzzy messaging, and no clear expectation for what success looks like. A better workflow is to define the audience, the promise, and the channel before you start creating. That does not mean every post needs ad spend. It means you should be able to explain why the piece deserves attention, whether that attention comes from organic reach, influencer distribution, email, or paid media. If you cannot name the outcome, you are probably making a nice asset instead of a useful growth tool.

## Influencers are not just top-of-funnel anymore

![A diverse set of creators holding phones while a customer journey map appears as a layered graphic behind them, modern flat illustration.](https://cdn.itwcreativeworks.com/newsletters/somiibo/content/-OzDmca_UyrA07weR_iz/section-2.png)

For a lot of brands, especially in older industries, influencer marketing still gets treated like a lightweight awareness play. That misses how people actually decide now. Social recommendations are showing up inside the purchase decision, not just before it. For younger audiences in particular, creator content is often more persuasive than a polished ad, which makes sense if you think about how they learn what to trust. The practical move is to stop asking whether influencers fit your brand and start asking where they fit in your customer journey. A creator clip can introduce the problem, compare options, or help close the sale. The format matters less than the role it plays.

## Legacy brands need social proof, not just polish

![A professional creator filming a straightforward testimonial-style video in a bright, minimal office setting, documentary-style illustration.](https://cdn.itwcreativeworks.com/newsletters/somiibo/content/-OzDmca_UyrA07weR_iz/section-3.png)

If you sell financial services, healthcare, insurance, or anything else that usually hides behind formal messaging, influencer content can feel awkward at first. That is usually a sign it is worth testing. People do not want less clarity from serious brands, but they do want proof that a product works in the real world and speaks in human language. The best creator partnerships in these categories are not flashy. They are specific, credible, and easy to repeat. Think of them as trust shortcuts. A good workflow is to start with one narrow use case, one audience segment, and one creator format, then measure whether the content moves people from curiosity to action.

## Design for the feed you are actually posting to

![A social media designer resizing one master graphic into multiple aspect ratios on several screens, crisp workspace illustration with motion lines showing adaptation.](https://cdn.itwcreativeworks.com/newsletters/somiibo/content/-OzDmca_UyrA07weR_iz/section-4.png)

A lot of social underperformance is just a sizing problem in disguise. If your visual is cropped badly, too text-heavy, or built for the wrong aspect ratio, the platform has to work harder than it should. That is a waste of effort on any channel, especially when you are repurposing content across several networks. The simplest fix is to build from a reusable master asset and export versions that fit each destination, with vertical formats prioritized for mobile feeds and stories. If you are managing this at scale, template-based resizing and a basic formatting checklist can save more time than another round of manual edits.
