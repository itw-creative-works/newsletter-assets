When your pages start fighting each other
That usually means the wrong page gets impressions, the right page gets buried, and conversions suffer because visitors land somewhere less useful.

A lot of teams create more content to win more search traffic, then accidentally end up with several pages chasing the same intent. Instead of sending authority to one strong page, you split it across near-duplicates and make it harder for search engines to decide what deserves to rank. The fix is simple in theory: map each page to a distinct intent, merge outdated pages into the strongest version, and stop publishing new variants unless they truly serve a different job.

More leads is not the same as better leads
If your ads platform only sees form fills, it will keep optimizing toward people who like filling out forms, not necessarily people who buy.

Paid search can fall into the same trap. That is how you end up with busy pipelines and weak revenue. Better lead quality comes from teaching the ad system what a valuable customer looks like. Connect your CRM, assign more value to downstream stages, and reward the conversions that actually turn into sales. Every channel needs a job description
Social, search, email, print, and QR should not all be judged by the same final metric at the same stage.

Multi-channel strategy gets messy when every channel is expected to do everything. One channel may be built for discovery, another for consideration, and another for closing. When the assignment is unclear, teams overreact to bad comparisons and underinvest in the parts of the journey that actually create momentum. Before launch, decide what each channel is supposed to do, what success looks like, and which metric proves it. That makes the reporting cleaner and the decisions less emotional.

Offline campaigns need better feedback loops
Flyers, menus, packaging, event signs, and business cards all create real-world attention, but without tracking, that traffic disappears into a gray area.

This is where QR codes can help tighten the loop between print and digital. A branded QR code gives you a measurable handoff from offline to online, and dynamic links let you change the destination without reprinting. The important part is not just counting scans. Tie each code to the campaign, location, or asset it came from so you can compare what drove real action, not just curiosity. That turns print from a guessing game into a channel you can actually optimize.
